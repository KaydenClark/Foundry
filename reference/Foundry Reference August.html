01

What it actually is

The Foundry is a portable harness — Markdown control documents, JSON contracts, and zero-dependency Node tools — that gives AI agents enough structure to change real systems without anyone losing track of who authorized what.

The organizing metaphor is a factory, and it isn't decorative. Work arrives at Intake. It gets routed by Orchestration, built in Forge or Production, judged by Assay, merged by Ward, and watched by Gauge. Every protected doorway between those rooms is a Gatehouse scan that writes an append-only receipt. Nothing about that is a scheduler or a runtime — it is a set of contracts and fail-closed rules that agents and deterministic scripts both have to satisfy.

The single load-bearing idea: no actor changes anything real without a Canon-backed Job Order that names the write scope. Not a user request, not a plan, not a green test, not a passing review. A Job Order, issued by a spec, scanned at every passage.

The distinction everything hangs on

A Job is the outcome you're asked to achieve. A Job Order is the authorization for what you may write. The Job says what to accomplish; the Job Order says what may change. Conflating them is the failure mode the whole apparatus exists to prevent.

04

Sockets and Modules

the plug boundary

A socket is a capability connection contract and nothing else — stable, capability-named, housed inside a Hall, registered in an open registry. A Module is a separately owned product that implements one. The Module can be swapped without renaming the socket; the contract travels with the Foundry, while the binding (which Module is plugged in, with what credentials) is instance data that never ships.

The rule that makes this real: no reach-arounds. An agent reaches a Module only through its declared entrypoint — reading the Module's files or database directly is not a valid connection. This is stated and enforced by review, not yet by machinery.

05

Six governance planes

where any fact lives

Every artifact in the system is classified by the role it plays, not by its file type. A spec is Canon. An audit report of that same spec is Grounding. A beautiful unadopted mockup is Grounding too. The classification determines what the artifact can and cannot authorize.

PLANE 1

Actuality

What is real — live filesystem, source, runtime, verified state. The most protected plane. Nothing changes it without a Canon-backed Job Order naming the write scope, and every change checks back in as Grounding.

PLANE 2

Canon

Approved durable operating truth: BLUEPRINT, stable specs, LEXICON, RUNBOOK, README, TASKBOARD. AGENTS.md is its narrower imperative layer. Only Canon issues a Job Order.

PLANE 3

Grounding

Verified observations, audit findings, test receipts, research, non-binding references. It supports, tests, and justifies Canon without becoming Canon — and never authorizes work.

PLANE 4

Enduring Context

Durable structure that informs and constrains: project structure, vocabulary, operating rules, ADRs. Constrains work; cannot authorize it.

PLANE 5

Intent

Pending actionable work — requests, locked decisions, deterministic actions. Triaged into a Job Order; not itself a command surface.

PLANE 6

Projection

The one-way interpretive lens onto Actuality. Written only by Pawns via render, project, mirror. Always an approximation — it records what state was when captured. Staleness fails closed.

A boundary that was corrected in Canon

The Wiki document store is not a governance plane. Individual notes may be classified as Enduring Context, but the store itself is a map and a highway — it routes agents to the owning planes without becoming one.

06

The Job Order and its seven-stage flight

The Job Order is the badge. Its owning spec issues the FUID and revision, intended result, responsible party, exact write scope, repositories, proof requirements, recovery path, exclusions, and landing destination. That issuance is the only grant. The Spec-local folder that holds the packet, context, claim, and append-only handoffs is Intent — it is not authority by virtue of existing.

Every Job Order runs the same ordered sequence. Each stage consumes the prior receipt, rechecks its own race-sensitive facts, and appends a new one. A handoff preserves context; it never grants authority.

01

/sitrep

Route one current Canon-backed Job Order and append the first bounded handoff. Opens no claim, grants no authority.

02

/preflight

Reconcile all six planes against live Actuality; prove the recovery, verification, landing, and closure paths exist. Emits pass, reconcile, blocked, or usage-error.

03

/launch-flight

A Steward-authenticated request presents one fact-bound Preflight receipt. Every bound fact and the exact lifecycle tip are reread immediately before the transition; any mismatch fails closed.

04

/in-flight

Work strictly inside accepted scope. Test, checkpoint, push an exact recoverable candidate. Scope growth returns to Canon issuance and a fresh Preflight — it is never absorbed.

05

/landing-check

An independent read-only reviewer judges the exact remote candidate SHA against the checked target. Any movement in either invalidates the verdict.

06

/land delivery, not completion

Declare stabilize, deliver, repair, or abort. Revalidate refs, retest the exact merge candidate, push with an explicit non-force refspec to integration only. Never advances main.

07

/postflight-check the only closure stage

Start from verified post-Land Actuality. Prove every plane represents it. Closes complete, blocked, aborted, delivered-unclosed, or recovery-required.

The fail-closed rule

Tooling is partial today. /launch-flight and /in-flight are installed but bootstrap-unverified; no mechanism yet performs deterministic close mutation. So an execution needing a missing stage fails closed and records the exact dependency rather than substituting an adjacent skill or describing the stage as done. A green check, a successful merge, or a satisfied reviewer never rounds up to a completed Job Order.

Clearance

a cap, never a grant

Clearance is a machine-enforced ceiling on what a Job Order may grant. It never replaces the Job Order and never widens one. The Gatehouse compiler intersects three things at every protected transition: the actor class cap, the exact Job Order scope, and the required owner gates. Denial produces an append-only redacted receipt.

08

FUID — permanent identity

Every entity carries an uppercase base36 identifier that is globally unique and never reused. Four characters for architecture — Halls, Modules, Sockets, Workshops. Six for work and runtime — Specs, Tickets, Intent requests, Job Orders, passage receipts. All-zero is reserved.

The identifier deliberately encodes nothing: no type, no parentage, no Hall affiliation. Those are registry fields. Typed IDs like P-005, S-027, TK-002 survive as unique lookup aliases — nothing was bulk-renamed, and history stays readable.

Allocation advances fixed-width (0009 → 000A, 000Z → 0010), persists one high-water mark per width, skips every active or retired value, and never happens in Recall or a projection. The portable registry owns product entities; a private instance registry owns Project and work-item allocations, and validation composes the two without publishing the private half.

Specs and Tickets also carry immutable Created and substantive Last worked dates. Claiming, closing, completing, and explicit content edits advance Last worked. Rendering, projecting, and polling deliberately do not — so a dashboard refresh can never make stale work look fresh.

The Schematic

a model of Canon, not of Actuality

Under Schematic/ sits a separately owned React + Vite application — 27 specs, 25 test files — that renders the Foundry as it will be once fully built. It opens on a rotatable factory campus: a six-storey Factory, its Halls, surrounding Module buildings, visible socket conduits, and a second Factory showing the same Foundry deployed on another host.

Its Workflow view plays the seven-stage flight as a deterministic simulation with a synchronized trace, receipts, clearance bands, and injectable trips. Scenarios can be duplicated, edited, exported, and reimported as validated JSON.

What it is careful not to be: the native product interface, CIC, a live Actuality mirror, or evidence that anything is implemented. Its runs are explanatory only, its state is browser-local, and it is excluded from the published product until named Proof and independent Assay gates pass.

Everything else in the tree

Roles/
Six legacy one-task authority contracts — Auditor, Captain, Chain Engineer, Designer, Planner, Scout. Loading a role means the current agent adopts that stance; it never spawns a sub-agent by itself. Engineer already migrated to the shared role-engineer skill.
Skills/
The generated skill catalog — the front doors (genesis, adoption, grilling, make-it-so, sitrep, implement) plus supporting disciplines. Availability is fail-closed: an unfinished rewrite is preserved in skills-pending/ but not invocable. Agent discovery points at the published catalog, not the live Forge checkout, so branch switches can't silently change what's available.
Projects/ Wiki/
Generic capability only — schema, templates, and tools. Enrollment is explicit; neither tool discovers arbitrary directories, and existing memory notes are never overwritten. Populated rooms and private notes are instance data.
Scheduled/Captain/
AFK policy and workflow mechanics. Level Zero has a hard property: no change must spawn zero models. Active schedule installation is always an instance operation, never implied by adoption.
templates/
ADOPTION.md, GENESIS.md, and instance binding/workflow scaffolds. Adoption validates the entire manifest before mutation, verifies native Halls in place, clones only declared Modules into ignored destinations, preserves existing files, and writes receipts outside product Git. A failed preflight leaves no partial clone.
reference/
Rendered schematic studies (HTML + PDF), atlas data, and the runtime verifier — design evidence, explicitly Grounding rather than Canon.
12

The honest read

The Foundry is, right now, a rigorously specified governance system with three working subsystems and a mostly unbuilt factory floor. That is not a criticism — it's the shape of the project, and the controls are unusually careful about saying so out loud.

What is genuinely real: the identity registry, the manifest and adoption machinery, the publication boundary, the spec workbench, the Captain scheduling primitives, and three folded-in products (Forge, Assay, Gauge) that predate the Hall architecture and do actual work. Three more Halls have one real primitive each — clearance policy, grounding journal, lifecycle transition. Ten of the thirteen Halls have no runtime.

What is declared but not implemented: the seven-stage flight as an executing pipeline, machine-enforced clearance at protected passages, the passage receipt trail, and the observation feeds. The Runbook is explicit that until the lifecycle engine exists, a normal Job Order that needs a missing stage must fail closed rather than pretend.

The risk worth naming: the control surface is very large relative to the running system, and Canon is amended often — the current branch carries 406 commits past main, and the recent history is dominated by revisions of Job Orders about Job Orders. The discipline that keeps this honest is also the discipline that makes it slow. The bet is that the discipline pays off once the Halls have something running in them.
