Plate 4 — For anyone standing at your shoulder

What am I looking at?

Imagine a factory that builds software instead of cars. You don't push buttons — you walk up to a door and say what you want out loud. A foreman inside hears you, flips on the lights, and a crew comes running: some read the instructions, some build, some inspect the work.

There are two of these factories — one on Kayden's Mac, one on the PC — and each has two doors: one crew is Codex, the other is Claude. The factories don't shout across the street to each other; they leave notes in a shared notebook (that's the cord between them) and read them when they get to it.

Bolted to the outside walls are helpers on wires: a memory clerk that fetches things fast, an intercom that can buzz Kayden's phone when something important happens, and a big mirror overhead that's meant to show everything going on at a glance.

The honest part: the mirror and the intercom aren't finished. That's why, when one crew quietly turned on a camera, Kayden only noticed by watching closely. Finishing them is exactly what's on the workbench now — so the building can tell you what it's doing, instead of you having to catch it.
